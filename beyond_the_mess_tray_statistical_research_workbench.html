01 • THE EVERYDAY HOSTEL STORY

Why Do We Skip Mess Meals We Already Paid For?

Every semester, university hostelers pre-pay for fixed dining contracts (7 lunches and 7 dinners a week). Yet, between evening lab fatigue, repetitive menu cycles, and roommate cravings, students routinely abandon the mess tray for night canteens and food delivery apps.

1. The 8 PM Mess Dilemma

After long lab hours, monotonous menus make walking to the dining hall feel like a chore—causing nearly 1 in 3 paid meals (32.6%) to go untouched.

2. Roommate Domino Effect

One roommate opens Zomato or heads to a night canteen. Within 5 minutes, the entire room pools an order (χ² = 33.28, p < 0.0001).

3. The Quiet Budget Drain

₹110 per skipped meal feels small in the moment—until ₹2,700+ vanishes from monthly pocket money on supplementary food.

Test 1: One-Sample t-Test vs. National Benchmark

Testing whether VIT weekly outside food spending ($\bar{Y} = \text{₹}685.82$) differs significantly from the national collegiate modal baseline ($\mu_0 = \text{₹}750/\text{wk}$, Saikia & Sarmah 2025).

μ₀ = ₹750 / wk
₹400 ₹589 (Lower 95% CI) ₹685.8 (Sample Ȳ) ₹750 (IJFMR) ₹1,000
t = Ȳ − μ0s / √n = 685.82 − 750713.89 / √208 = -64.1849.50 = -1.2966
df = n − 1 = 207 SE = ₹49.50 tcrit, 0.05 = ±1.9715 p-value = 0.1962
Hypotheses: H₀: μ = ₹750/wk  vs.  H₁: μ ≠ ₹750/wk (Two-Tailed, α = 0.05)
✅ Statistical Verdict: Fail to Reject H₀ (p = 0.1962 > 0.05)

Because |t| = 1.2966 < 1.9715, the ₹64.18 difference is well within normal sampling variation. Campus outside food spending is statistically consistent with national Indian university pocket-money norms!

Slide 7 (Right) • Welch Two-Sample t-Test

Test 2: Welch Two-Sample t-Test & Satterthwaite df

Comparing two groups with unequal sample sizes and unequal variances ($s_1^2 \neq s_2^2$) without pooling variances.

t = Ȳ₁ − Ȳ₂√( s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂ ) = 647.01 − 1800.00√( 666.77²/201 + 1255.32²/7 ) = -2.4185
Welch–Satterthwaite df: df = (s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂)²(s₁²/n₁)²/(n₁−1) + (s₂²/n₂)²/(n₂−1) = 6.13  |  p = 0.0512

⚠️ Statistical Verdict: Marginally Significant (t = -2.4185, df = 6.13, p = 0.0512)

Day Scholars spend nearly 3× more outside (₹1,800 vs ₹647/wk) because they buy daily retail lunches without a subsidized hostel mess card. Because n₂ = 7 has high variance (s₂²/7 = 225,118), Satterthwaite pulls df down close to n₂ − 1 = 6.

# BAMAT207 Lab Project: Complete Statistical Analysis (N=208)

# =========================================================
library(readxl)

df <- read_excel("Campus Dining Behavior, Mess Satisfaction & Expenditure Study (BAMAT207).xlsx")
colnames(df)[2:6] <- c("Arrangement", "Skips_Raw", "Spend_Raw", "Rating", "Peer")

# 1. Class Midpoint Transformations: xi = (Li + Ui) / 2
df$Skips <- ifelse(grepl("0 - 2", df$Skips_Raw), 1.0,
            ifelse(grepl("3 - 5", df$Skips_Raw), 4.0,
            ifelse(grepl("6 - 8", df$Skips_Raw), 7.0,
            ifelse(grepl("9 - 11", df$Skips_Raw), 10.0, 13.0))))

df$Spend <- ifelse(grepl("0 - 200|0 - ₹200", df$Spend_Raw), 100,
            ifelse(grepl("201", df$Spend_Raw), 350,
            ifelse(grepl("501", df$Spend_Raw), 750,
            ifelse(grepl("1,001|1001", df$Spend_Raw), 1250,
            ifelse(grepl("1,501|1501", df$Spend_Raw), 2000, 3000)))))
df$Rating <- as.numeric(df$Rating)

# 2. Theme 1: Descriptive Moments & Pearson Correlations
mean(df$Spend); sd(df$Spend); median(df$Spend)
cor(df[, c("Spend", "Skips", "Rating")])

# 3. Theme 2: Binomial Fit X ~ B(14, p_hat)
p_hat <- mean(df$Skips) / 14 # 0.3259
14 * p_hat * (1 - p_hat)     # Theoretical Var = 3.08 vs s^2 = 10.60

# 4. Theme 3: Hypothesis Tests (t-tests & Chi-Square)
t.test(df$Spend, mu = 750) # 1-Sample vs IJFMR (2025) Benchmark
df$Group2 <- ifelse(grepl("Hosteler", df$Arrangement), "Hostel", "Day")
t.test(Spend ~ Group2, data = df) # Welch 2-Sample (df = 6.13)
chisq.test(table(df$Arrangement, trimws(df$Peer))) # Chi-Sq = 33.28

# 5. Theme 4 & 5: Multiple Regression & One-Way ANOVA
summary(lm(Spend ~ Skips + Rating, data = df)) # R^2 = 0.420
anova_fit <- aov(Spend ~ as.factor(Arrangement), data = df)
summary(anova_fit); TukeyHSD(anova_fit)
